Kindred are an ancient line of vampires who have existed since the first days of humanity. These vampires are innately social creatures who form their own socio-political sects and divisions, often based on clans and bloodlines. They gather for camaraderie and safety among the only beings who can understand the eternal hunger within.

Kindred draw their abilities, collectively known as vampiric Disciplines, from the supernatural power in their blood, which they more eloquently call vitae. The older and more powerful Kindred become, the more blood they can consume and concentrate within themselves, calling upon it to fuel their undead bodies and powers. These Disciplines allow them to do unbelievable things. They can possess intense strength, durability, or speed. They can transform into creatures of the night, or even mist. They can leash the wills of others. They can manipulate minds and perceptions. And they can reshape the darkness in which they are at home. Kindred can call upon truly terrifying powers, and they are all powered by vitae.

Kindred can develop in different ways, but as the centuries of their existence have proven, the clans and bloodlines carry specific aspects of their vampirism throughout the generations, from sire to childe. These manifest as each clan’s ancient bane, the cost to be paid beyond just the endless hunger for blood and the struggle to keep at bay the Beast inside. There are Kindred in every walk of life, it seems, hiding in plain sight, acting like mortals, and maintaining their eternal Masquerade.

## Becoming a Kindred …

#### As a Level 1 Character

- Gain all the traits in the Core Kindred Traits table.
- Gain the Kindred’s level 1 features, which are listed in the Kindred Features table.

#### As a Multiclass Character

- Gain the following traits from the Core Kindred Traits table: Hit Point Die, proficiency in one skill of your choice from the Kindred’s skill list, and training with Light Armor and [[Breastplate]].
- Gain the Kindred’s level 1 features, which are listed in the Kindred Features table.

> [!recite|no-i] Kindred and Other Species
> In the _Vampire: The Masquerade_ (and the collective _World of Darkness_) setting, the vast majority of Kindred are human. While there might be the occasional plot-based variation, all Kindred characters are made using the human species.
>
> In fifth edition games, however, since Kindred is a class, other species can be Kindred if GMs want to create vampiric options in their campaigns.

### Level 1: Feed

You feed on the blood of other creatures. This is most often accomplished through your sharp fangs. As an action, you can feed on another creature within 5 feet of yourself if the target is willing, [[Charmed]] by you, or has the [[Incapacitated]], [[Grappled]], [[Paralyzed]], [[Restrained]], [[Stunned]], or [[Unconscious]] condition. You can feed only once per turn.

When you feed, roll up to your maximum Feed Dice plus your Constitution modifier (minimum of 1). The creature takes Necrotic damage equal to the roll’s total. This damage only reduces the creature’s Hit Point maximum by an amount equal to the roll’s total. The number of Feed Dice increases as you gain Kindred levels, as shown in the Feed Dice column of the [[#Kindred Features]] table. If you roll a 6 on a d6 on a Feed Die, you can regain 1 expended Blood Point. If a creature’s Hit Point maximum is reduced to 0, you can regain 1 expended Blood Point.

**_Feeding from the Willing._** If you feed on a willing creature or a creature [[Charmed]] by you, it can spend 1 [[Hit Point Dice|Hit Point Die]]. It rolls the Hit Point Die and reduces its Hit Point maximum by the amount rolled, and you can regain 1 expended Blood Point. Feeding in this way doesn’t end the Charmed condition.

**_Hit Die Deficit._** If a creature has no remaining Hit Point Dice, the creature takes Necrotic damage equal to a roll of your Feed Dice plus your Constitution modifier, and the creature’s Hit Point maximum is reduced by an amount equal to the roll’s total.

**_Maximum Successful Feedings._** A creature can provide a number of Blood Points based on its size. A Small creature provides 1 Blood Point, a Medium provides 3, a Large provides 5, a Huge provides 7, and a Gargantuan provides 10. A creature regains all lost Blood Points when it finishes a [[Long Rest]].

**_Creature Susceptible to Feed._** You can feed on any creature with blood or other life-giving fluid. Aberrations, Beasts, Dragons, Fey, Giants, Monstrosities, and Humanoids are the most common targets. Constructs, Elementals, Oozes, and Plants usually don’t have blood. Work with your GM to determine the types of creatures you can feed on.

> [!recite|no-i] The Blood Bond
> A mysterious power in the blood of Kindred causes creatures to become Blood Bound to that Kindred when:
>
> - They drink from the same Kindred at least three times within one year.
> - At least one [[Long Rest]] occurs between each of the three drinks.
>
> While Blood Bound to a Kindred, a creature is considered [[Charmed]] by that Kindred and has [[Advantage]] on saving throws to avoid being Charmed or [[Frightened]] by other creatures. This is in addition to the effects from the _[[Kindred Vitae]]_ consumable.
>
> A Blood Bond can be suppressed for 1 minute if the creature succeeds on a DC 25 Charisma saving throw. The bond is only permanently removed if the creature doesn’t drink from that Kindred again for a year, or if the Kindred in question is killed.

### Level 1: The Beast

You have a terrible Beast within that can only be sated with blood. As a [[Bonus Action]], you can use this Beast’s predatory instincts to sharpen your senses and abilities. Until the start of your next turn, you have [[Advantage]] on [[D20 Test|D20 Tests]] you make.

You can use this feature a number of time equal to your Proficiency Bonus, and you regain all expended uses when you finish a [[Short Rest|Short]] or [[Long Rest]].

**_Hunger._** If a D20 Test benefiting from your Beast feature fails, you go into a bloodlusted, hunger-driven state. This bloodlusted state ends after 1 minute or after you have successfully used your Feed feature, whichever comes first.

While bloodlusted, you regard the creature nearest to you that you can see or hear and that you can feed on as your prey. If there are multiple possible creatures, choose one at random. On each of your turns, you must move as close to the creature as possible and take the [[Attack]] action to make an [[Unarmed Strike]] to grapple the target. If you’re unable to get close enough to the creature to grapple it, your turn ends after you’ve used up all your available movement. If the creature dies or can no longer be seen or heard by you, the next nearest creature that you can see or hear and that you can feed on becomes your new target. If the target is in range and has the [[Grappled]] condition at the start of your turn, you use your action to feed on it.

### Level 1: Blood Potency

You are fueled by blood, represented by Blood Points. Your Kindred level determines the number of points you have, as shown in the Blood Points column of the [[#Kindred Features]] table. You can expend Blood Points in the following ways:

**_Heal Wounds._** As a [[Bonus Action]], you can expend 1 Blood Point to regain Hit Points equal to 1d10 plus your Kindred level.

**_Hunger Sated._** If you fail a [[D20 Test]] that is affected by your Beast feature, you can expend 1 Blood Point to ignore the effects of Hunger.

**_Vitae Powers._** There are [[Feats.base#Kindred Feats|Kindred feats]] and subclass features fueled by expending Blood Points.

### Level 1: Kindred Biology

You are a specific kind of vampire known as a Kindred, which brings physical benefits and drawbacks.

**_Awaken._** You don’t regain expended Blood Points when you finish a [[Short Rest|Short]] or [[Long Rest]]. To gain the benefits of a Long Rest, you must have at least 1 Blood Point in your pool or you only gain the benefits of a Short Rest.

**_Darkvision._** You have [[Darkvision]] with a range of 60 feet.

**_Sanguivore._** You can’t eat food or drink. If you consume more than a nibble or sip, you have the [[Poisoned]] condition that ends once you expel what you consumed. While Poisoned in this way, you must succeed on a Constitution saving throw every 10 minutes to avoid having the [[Incapacitated]] condition, which lasts for 1 minute as you vomit up what you’ve consumed. The initial DC of this saving throw is 10, and it increases by 5 with each successive save.

Instead, you must consume a number of Blood Points per day equal to the number of pounds of food a creature your size normally requires per day (usually, 1 per day for Small and Medium creatures). You can expend 1 Blood Point to automatically succeed on the DC 10 Constitution saving throw to avoid gaining 1 level of [[Exhaustion]] from [[Malnutrition]].

Expending Blood Points to activate your Kindred features or [[Feats.base#Kindred Feats|Kindred Feats]] does not count towards the Blood Points you need to expend for sustenance.

**_Sunlight Vulnerability._** You take 5 Radiant damage if you start your turn in sunlight.

**_Supernatural Spellcasting._** Charisma is your spellcasting ability for spells from your Kindred subclass and Kindred feats, your spells require no Verbal or Somatic components, and they require no Material components unless they are consumed by the spell or have a cost specified in it, taking damage can’t break your [[Concentration]].

**_Torpor._** If your [[Exhaustion]] level is 6, you aren’t killed. Instead, you drop to 1 Hit Point, become [[Unconscious]], and can’t remove the condition until you have expended a number of Blood Points equal to your Proficiency Bonus over any length of time. Another creature can feed you while you are Unconscious to recover Blood Points but the details of how that occurs is left for you and your GM to determine.

**_Undead._** You have the Undead (Kindred) creature type in addition to your normal creature type. You don’t age.

**_Vampiric Resilience._** You automatically succeed on [[Death Saving Throws]]. Being reduced to 0 Hit Points by Fire or Radiant damage, or being beheaded kills you outright.

**_Vampiric Vulnerabilities._** You have [[Vulnerability]] to Fire and Radiant damage.

**_Wooden Stake Through the Heart._** When you are attacked with a wooden weapon that deals Piercing damage and you are reduced to 0 Hit Points or the attack roll is a [[Critical Hit]], you are staked through the heart and have the [[Paralyzed]] condition until the weapon is removed.

### Level 2: Kindred Feat

You gain a [[Feats.base#Kindred Feats|Kindred feat]] of your choice for which you qualify. You gain this feature again at Kindred levels 7, 10, 13, and 17.

### Level 3: Kindred Subclass

You gain a [[#Kindred Subclasses|Kindred subclass]] of your choice. A subclass is a specialization that grants you features at certain Kindred levels. For the rest of your career, you gain each of your subclass’s features that are of your Kindred level or lower.

> [!recite|no-i] Creating Kindred Characters in the World of Darkness
> In the _Vampire: The Masquerade_, Kindred are usually aware of their clan or bloodline soon after they are sired. In order to maintain game balance with other classes, levels 1 and 2 of the Kindred class are designed to represent the learning curve of becoming Kindred, a process that can take longer for some.
>
> In a campaign set wholly in the _World of Darkness_, it is suggested that Kindred characters be created at level 3. This allows them to choose their clan or bloodline from the beginning of the game, and this may allow them to create lengthier backstories that can bring their _Vampire: The Masquerade_ characters to life, proverbially speaking.

### Level 4: Ability Score Improvement

You gain the [[Ability Score Improvement]] feat or another [[Feats.base|feat]] of your choice for which you qualify. You gain this feature again at Kindred levels 8, 12, and 16.

### Level 5: Bestial Fury

Once per turn when you hit a target with a Melee weapon or [[Unarmed Strike]], you can expend a use of your Beast feature to roll the weapon’s damage dice twice and use either roll against the target. If this damage reduces it to 0 Hit Points, you can’t choose to leave the creature at 1 Hit Point as per the “Knocking Out a Creature” rule.

**_Bestial Flurry._** While affected by your Beast feature, you can attack twice instead of once whenever you take the [[Attack]] action on your turn.

### Level 5: Improved Feed

You can use the Feed feature as a [[Bonus Action]]. In addition, a creature [[Grappled]] by you has [[Disadvantage]] on its checks to escape the grapple.

### Level 10: Vampiric Will

You have [[Advantage]] on saving throws to avoid or end the [[Charmed]] condition, and against effects that turn undead.

### Level 14: Efficient Feeding

Whenever you roll a die for your Feed feature, you can reroll the die, and you must use the new roll.

### Level 19: Epic Boon

You gain an [[Feats.base#Epic Boon Feats|Epic Boon feat]] or another [[Feats.base|feat]] of your choice for which you qualify.

### Level 20: Ancient Awakening

The essence of the original vampiric Antediluvians has awoken in you. Pick four Ability Scores. Increase each chosen Ability Score by 2, to a maximum of 25.

If a Kindred subclass feature allows for an Ability Score to be increased beyond 25, Ancient Awakening can increase that Ability Score up to the maximum noted in that other feature.

> [!recite|no-i] Kindred or Cainite?
> In the Dark Ages setting, vampires do not yet call themselves Kindred. That term belongs to a later era, born from the political institutions that will eventually organize vampiric society across the modern nights. In the Dark Ages, vampires more commonly call themselves Cainites, tracing their lineage to Caine, the first of their kind.
>
> The Kindred class is designed to work across any era in which vampires have existed. In modern nights, “Cainite” carries specific weight as the preferred term of the Sabbat sect, and using it as a catch-all can create confusion. Kindred is used here as more neutral term for the class.
>
> If you are playing in a Dark Ages campaign, feel free to have your character use Cainite at the table. The rules work the same either way. What you are, regardless of the century, is something that drinks blood and does not die easily.

## Kindred Subclasses

A Kindred subclass is a specialization that grants you features at certain Kindred levels, as specified in the subclass. These subclasses represent your vampiric clan or bloodline.

**Note:** Kindred subclasses give you a subclass-specific Bane feature. This is a thematic weakness attached to that specific clan or bloodline.
